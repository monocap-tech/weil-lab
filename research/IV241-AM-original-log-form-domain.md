## 1. Exact Gamma growth from actual gain series
Write gamma(r)=Re psi(1/4+ir/2)-log pi and gamma0=gamma(0). The source gain series including n=0 is gamma(r)-gamma0=sum_{n>=0} G_r(n+1/4), G_r(x)=t^2/[x(x^2+t^2)]=1/x-x/(x^2+t^2), t=|r|/2. For x>0 the gain is nonnegative decreasing (denominator increasing), integrable at infinity, zero for t=0. Integral comparison gives
  integral_{1/4}^infinity G_r(x) dx <= sum <= G_r(1/4)+integral_{1/4}^infinity G_r(x) dx.
The integral is (1/2)log(1+16t^2)=(1/2)log(1+4r^2), by antiderivative log x-(1/2)log(x^2+t^2); G_r(1/4)<=4. Hence
  (1/2)log(1+4r^2) <= gamma(r)-gamma0 <= (1/2)log(1+4r^2)+4.
In particular 0<=gamma(r)-gamma0-(1/2)log(1+r^2)<=4+log 2. The last follows (1+4r^2)/(1+r^2)<=4. This is an analytic consequence of the literal imported series, not a new Lean theorem. The existing source proves gamma0> -28/5.

## 3. Logarithmic form domain and bounded perturbations

This is supported on [-2s,2s], Fourier sqrt(s) fhat(sr). Dilation is strongly continuous in weighted Fourier norm as s->1: for s in [1/2,1] change variables and use log(1+(r/s)^2)<=log(1+r^2)+log4 to obtain uniformly bounded maps; first check convergence on continuous compactly supported Fourier functions by dominated convergence, then approximate in weighted L2. Convolve f_s with a smooth compactly supported approximate identity of radius <2(1-s). The result is smooth compactly supported inside I. Its Fourier multiplier is bounded by 1 and converges pointwise to 1, giving D convergence for fixed s. Choose a diagonal sequence.

Gamma bound shows A=(1/2)L plus bounded real multiplier form (gamma0 plus gain remainder), hence its natural domain is D. Prime P is bounded on supported L2 by finite translations and positive finite coefficients; stronger |P|<=C||f||^2, C=7.979648, extends from IV238's analytic row bound by L2 density and continuity, independent of terminal binary. ## 5. A genuine finite-complement receiver (not a practical bank)
For a finite partition of I into N equal cells of length d=4/N, let U_N be their indicator span. Indicators and u-v (zero extended) are in D by the same finite derivative measure argument. Let F=U_N+span(u-v). For f in D orthogonal in L2 to F, Pi>=0 and f has zero integral on each cell. For R>0 let P_R be the full-line orthogonal Fourier low-pass projection. For any z with ||z||=1, P_R z has derivative norm <=R. Subtract cell averages from P_R z on I; interval Poincare inequality gives norm <=(d/pi)R. Since f is orthogonal to the cell averages, duality yields ||P_R f||<=d R/pi ||f||. Thus the exact gain bound gives
  Q(f) >= [gamma0-C+(1/2)log(1+4R^2)*(1-(dR/pi)^2)]||f||^2
when dR/pi<=1. Example existence witness only: R=2^23, N=2^25, dR=1. With pi>3, log2>2/3 (e.g. log2=2*atanh(1/3)>2/3), gamma0>-28/5 and C<8, coefficient > -68/5+(8/9)*16=28/45. No matrix or bank of this size is generated. This enormous witness is a proof of finite complement, not an efficient proposed computational strategy.

Let D_perp=D intersect F^perp. The positive complement Q>=eta||.||^2 there together with global Q>=L/2-D0||.||^2 yields Q>=c||.||_D^2 for some c>0 by convex combination (D0=max(0,C+S-4-gamma0); take theta=eta/[2(eta+D0+1)] for weight on global lower bound). Hence on D_perp the polarized Q is a bounded coercive Hermitian inner product equivalent to D norm. For every v in F there is unique z_v in D_perp with Q(z_v,h)=Q(v,h) for all h in D_perp (Riesz; choose consistent conjugate-linear first convention). For f=v+h, completing the square gives Q(f)=Q(v)-Q(z_v)+Q(h+z_v). The finite Schur form Q(v)-Q(z_v) is necessary and sufficient for positivity of Q on D. This is a mathematically exact reduction, not mere compression to F or a positivity claim. Its dimension bound is deliberately crude and the inverse complement not yet certified. Alternative abstract compactness: bounded sets in D have Fourier tails uniformly small, and restriction to I of the lowpass map on I is Hilbert-Schmidt, giving compact embedding into L2(I). No infinite matrix computation needed for this claim.
