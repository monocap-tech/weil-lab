# RPB108: prime-power-8 target and aperture-dependent certification criterion

Additive definitions, 2026-10-08 UTC (2026-10-07 Pacific).

The target is a=21/20, d=2a=21/10. Active arithmetic terms are prime powers n with log(n)<d; their per-orientation translation amplitude is Lambda(n)/sqrt(n). In particular Lambda(8)=log(2), not log(8). Translation source coordinates are t=(x+a)/(2a). The two support events for n are t=log(n)/d and t=1-log(n)/d.

E_k is the physical L2 orthonormal span of degrees 0,...,k-1. F_k is its physical mass-orthogonal complement in the canonical supported form domain. A complement certificate is a lower bound c(a,k)>0 on Q restricted to F_k, not positivity of Q on the whole domain.

For the complete actual source operator on E_k, B=(I-P_E)J|E_k. A surrogate B_tilde with operator error <=eta gives actual Gram upper B*B <= R_tilde+delta I, delta=eta(2M+eta), M>=||B_tilde||. The corrected finite sufficient inequality is Q_E-c^-1(R_tilde+delta I)>=tau I with tau>0. Failure of this inequality does not assert a negative actual Q vector.

An effective specified-aperture criterion returns rigorously enclosed data and verifies those inequalities for a supplied aperture. An all-aperture theorem verifies adequate data for every finite aperture, or gives a lawful global continuation argument. These are distinct statements.

The positive-Bessel three-band method ceiling is an upper bound on the LOWER BOUND produced by that particular comparison, not an upper bound on actual complement coercivity. The Bernoulli geometric guard failure is a failure of the present remainder proof, not a singularity of the actual real-axis form.
