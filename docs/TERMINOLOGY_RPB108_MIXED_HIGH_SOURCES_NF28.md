# NF28 terminology — complete mixed high-source probe

Definitions are additive; historical wording is unchanged.

Let E be the original E112 retained space, F=E-perp, v the exact NF26
trial and w the frozen exact NF27 retained response in one physical
parity. Its retained seed x satisfies x*w=0 exactly.

The **complete mixed high-source Gram** is the physical L2 Gram of
r_v=P_F L_a v and r_w=P_F L_a w. Both columns include all original arch,
prime and signed-pole sectors, their crosses, and endpoint logarithms.
It is a two-direction probe, not the full retained source Gram.

Let Q2 be the original native form on (v,w), Gamma their complete high
Gram, and kappa=207/1000 the original full high-floor certificate. The
**two-direction sufficient collective matrix** is U2=Q2-Gamma/kappa.
If U2 is positive definite, the original Schur restriction on the
retained span(x,w) is positive, including the full infinite high space.
A rigorously nonpositive U2 rejects this sufficient estimator only; it
is not a negative direction of the original form.

The **native-to-high correction loss** on w is ||r_w||²/kappa. Comparing
it with Q(w,w) tests whether the finite retained response alone has a
positive scalar sufficient score. The mixed phase <r_v,r_w> is required
for arbitrary combinations of the two columns.

A successful mixed probe would control this specific retained plane.
It would not supply positivity of the remaining 54 retained directions
per parity or the whole-domain sign. A failed probe identifies additional
source or high-response information needed for collective assembly.

If the fixed w in W violates ||P_F Lw||²<kappa Q(w), the full coarse
collective matrix with the other retained columns left unlifted cannot
be positive definite: its quadratic form on w is already negative.
Computing more entries or changing the basis of that fixed trial space
cannot repair this sufficient matrix. Additional high lifts or a stronger
original high-response estimate change the test; no claim about the sign
of the original collective Schur form follows from this obstruction.
