# RPB108: real-zero division and jump-test custody

Additive definitions, 2026-10-07. Use the complete native form Q, linear in its first argument, and the physical Fourier convention from KERNEL_DE_BRANGES_ATTACHMENT. At nonnegative contact K has dimension r, top generator h_* in H^(r-1), and F_h*=Phi up to a fixed scalar phase. Its global H^r flag is zero.

For a real zero t of Phi, u_t is the same-window solution (D+it)u_t=h_*, with Fourier profile i Phi(z)/(z-t). A removable evaluation at z=t is i Phi'(t). These division vectors are tests in the canonical logarithmic domain; they are not presumed to be kernel vectors.

For arbitrary complex w, J_w is the supported, single-interior-jump test with Fourier profile i(Phi(z)-Phi(w))/(z-w). In physical coordinates the jump is placed at x=0. Its distributional equation is (D+iw)J_w=h_*-Phi(w)delta_0. It belongs to the logarithmic form domain, but its derivative generally does not.

A positive test Gram is G(w,v)=Q(J_w,J_v). For nonreal w normalize k_w=J_w/Phi(w) and H(w,v)=Q(k_w,k_v). The differences k_w-k_v have zero jump and lawful derivatives. Their full-null skew identity gives H(w,v)=(M(w)-overline(M(v)))/(w-overline(v)), with M fixed up to a real constant. This is the actual scalar Pick representation. Its source profile i[Phi(z)/Phi(w)-1]/(z-w) has a removable apparent pole at every nonreal source node; its correction must be retained when checking poles and residues.
