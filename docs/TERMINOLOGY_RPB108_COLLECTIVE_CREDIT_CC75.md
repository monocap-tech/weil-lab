# CC75 — collective inverse-response credit

Definitions are additive; historical entries are unchanged. All actual source quantities here concern the original Weil form at aperture 53/50. The fixed pair and remaining probe have NF32's meanings, and the high floor is kappa=207/1000.

On any fixed finite trial family L, D=L*QL is its finite energy matrix, R=P_F QL is its complete high source, Gamma=R*R is the complete physical source Gram, and C=Q|F is the actual high operator. S=D-Gamma/kappa is the scalar-floor sufficient matrix. Delta=Gamma/kappa-R*C^-1R is the positive semidefinite inverse-response credit. The actual retained Schur matrix is exactly S+Delta.

The minimal collective credit inequality for positivity on this family is Delta > -S as a matrix inequality. For quantitative physical mass M>0, the corresponding condition is Delta+S >= epsilon M for epsilon>0. An entrywise collection of strict diagonal credits is insufficient. If D>0, congruence by D^-1/2 is an equivalent native-energy normalization, not a new source improvement. Any invertible coordinate change preserves the inertia of S and of S+Delta.

For a fixed remaining probe u with native energy q and complete source square g, a positive actual Schur value requires Delta(u,u)>g/kappa-q. Its required fraction of floor response is 1-kappa q/g. These are necessary scalar conditions; they are not certified actual credits, sufficient collective conditions, or universal requirements for changed high-lift families.

An inverse-response crossing control is an exact finite block with D=I, R=I and a positive high operator satisfying the stated floor, whose actual Schur determinant crosses zero while every scalar credit target remains strictly satisfied. Such controls test the proposed matrix implication. They are not arithmetic Weil countermodels.
