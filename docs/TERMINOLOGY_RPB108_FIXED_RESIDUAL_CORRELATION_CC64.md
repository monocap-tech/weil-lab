# CC64 terminology: fixed-trial high-source correlation

These definitions are additive; read them before the CC64 report.

At aperture a=53/50, E=E112 is the retained physical Legendre space,
F=E-perp the full canonical high complement, and C=C_full its original
high form/operator, with C>=kappa I, kappa=207/1000.

A **fixed trial p** is exactly the CC63 rational polynomial, including its
two rational high coefficients. It is not the different denominator10^80
NF24 target and not an exact Galerkin minimizer.

H denotes the physical isometry onto the two measured high polynomials.
C2=H*CH is their original native block. **Complete high trial source**
r=P_F Lp and **complete measured high sources** G=P_F LH belong to physical
L2. Both projections remove all retained coordinates, not only two.

**Measured residual u** is H*r=Q(H,p). It is retained explicitly even when
its certified intervals are tiny.

**Fixed-trial source Gram data** are P=r*r (scalar for one trial),
D=G*G (two-by-two), and z=G*r (two-vector), using all signed original source
sectors before squaring. A three-source joint Gram suffices for these
directional data; it does not establish a collective56-direction gate.

**Correlation matrix and corrected correlation** are
V=D-kappa C2=C2(C2-kappa I)+K*K and zbar=z-kappa u,
where K=P_(F minus H)CH. V is strictly positive from C2>kappa.

**Correlation credit** is zbar*V^-1 zbar. The sharp partial-data response
majorant is (P-credit)/kappa. Sharpness refers only to the listed source
columns, C2, and scalar high floor, not to all original Weil identities.

A **paid rational correlation trial Y** is a fixed two-vector for which
the complete physical residual r-GY and the original finite correction
2 Re(u*Y)-Y*C2Y are enclosed. Its upper bound is
||r-GY||²/kappa+2 Re(u*Y)-Y*C2Y.

**Native Bessel lower certificate** sums measured high coordinate squares.
It proves a lower bound on ||r||², never an upper bound on inverse response.
**Numerical diagnostic** has no rigorous integration remainder and cannot
certify failure of a sufficient gate, negativity, or a true null.
