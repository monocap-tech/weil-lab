# RPB108 good-height contour reduction

Registered 2026-10-07 UTC before use.

- F is any finite family in the actual canonical supported logarithmic domain D_a. Taking a physical orthonormal basis of the full unshifted contact kernel gives F=K. No nullity is built into the generic definitions.
- H_h(s)=integral h(x)exp((s-1/2)x)dx and W_F(s)=sum_h H_h(s)conjugate(H_h(1-conjugate(s))). These are entire functions. Conjugating coefficients, rather than the variable, makes the second factor holomorphic.
- xi(s)=s(s-1)pi^(-s/2)Gamma(s/2)zeta(s)/2 is the completed entire function; A(s)=xi'(s)/xi(s), r(s)=-i(s-1/2), c=3/2 and delta=c-1/2=1.
- Good heights E avoid every actual positive zero ordinate by eta/log T, with fixed sufficiently small eta. E has uniformly positive measure in each sufficiently large unit interval.
- L_F(T)=integral_(1-c)^c sum_h (|H_h(sigma+iT)|^2+|H_h(sigma-iT)|^2)d sigma is a strip energy. It is not a sharp zero head.
- B_F(T)=(1/pi) integral_(t0)^T Re[r(c+it)W_F(c+it)A(c+it)-r(c-it)W_F(c-it)A(c-it)]dt. This includes both actual vertical contour sides via functional-equation reflection. The fixed t0>2 avoids all zero ordinates.
- The good-height theorem concerns horizontal edges tending to zero. It does not assert boundedness of B_F or S_F.
