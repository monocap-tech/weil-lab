# RPB108 terminology: critical joint boundary matching

Date: 2026-10-07 UTC. Additive global/F4 registry.

- The right edge profile is f(v)=h(a-v), 0<v<2a, for the unchanged actual physical h.
- Its full Carleman profile is Cf(u)=integral_0^(2a) f(v)/(u+v)dv, u>0. This integral is absolutely defined at every positive u by L2 and finite support.
- Critical joint boundary matching is the weighted L2 conclusion integral_0^delta |Cf(u)-2A_h(u)|^2/[u log(1/u)]du<infinity. It retains the entire actual drive and Carleman profile, not one scalar observation.
- The signed inverse boundary moment M_R(h) is the limit as epsilon decreases to zero of integral_epsilon^(2a) h(a-v)/v dv, only when this complex improper limit exists. Absolute integrability is a separate assertion.
- The logarithmic primitive coordinate is L=log(1/v), with F(L)=f(exp(-L)); the second coordinate is z=log L. These are proof coordinates, not physical vector transport.
- Critical boundary moment matching is M_R(h)=2A_h(0). It is not an injectivity assertion and does not identify h from M_R.
- A matched-cusp comparison uses a supported v^(1/4) profile and an independently assigned constant drive equal to half its inverse moment. Its drive is not asserted to be the actual native A_h; it is not an actual null vector.
