### AZ-MIXED-CAUCHY-ALIGN

For every sufficiently high admitted selected packet \(F\in U_*\), a canonical
family of derivative zeros

\[
\Tau(F)=\{\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_m\}
\]

must satisfy, on the zero-moment/KPH-soft sector,

\[
\boxed{
\|D_{\Tau,F}v\|\ge H_F^{-a}

\[
\boxed{
\|D_{\Tau,F}v\|
\le

This screen searches only for theorem classes capable of supplying one of those

## 1. Garaev--Yildirim remains the strongest unconditional pointwise direction

Garaev and Yildirim prove unconditionally that for every zero

\[
\rho'=\beta'+i\gamma'
\]

of \(\zeta'\), there exists a zero

\[
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
\]

of \(\zeta\) such that

\[
|\gamma-\gamma'|
\ll
\sqrt{|\beta'-1/2|}.
\]

This is a genuine pointwise relation between the two divisors.

But its quantifier is

\[
\boxed{
\forall\rho'\in Z(\zeta')
\ \exists\rho\in Z(\zeta).
}
\]

IRT-1B needs the reverse packet-conditioned direction:

\[
\boxed{
\forall F\text{ dangerous}
\ \exists\Tau(F)\subset Z(\Xi')
\text{ forming a conditioned frame}.
}
\]

The theorem does not imply surjectivity from prescribed zeta packets to
derivative zeros and gives neither row multiplicity nor conditioning.

**Disposition:** POINTWISE / WRONG DIRECTION / NO DD-FRAME.

Primary source:
M. Z. Garaev and C. Y. Yildirim,
*On small distances between ordinates of zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) and
\(\zeta'(s)\)*, IMRN 2007, arXiv:math/0610377.

## 2. Reverse zeta-to-derivative proximity exists only in restricted/RH settings

The closest reverse-direction theorem located is the Zhang/Soundararajan
near-critical-line lineage.

A representative theorem states, under RH, that if two consecutive zeta zeros
have a sufficiently small normalized gap, then there is a zero of \(\zeta'\)
within \(O(1/\log T)\) of the zeta zero.

This does begin from zeta-zero geometry, but it fails IRT admission for several
reasons:

1. RH is assumed;
2. it applies to a special small-gap configuration, not every dangerous
   CF-A17-type packet;
3. it supplies one derivative zero, not the \(n-1\) frame or a one-ray
   projectively nonorthogonal observation;
4. it gives no Cauchy--Vandermonde lower bound;
5. it gives no weighted alignment with the KPH residual.

Therefore it cannot be used inside the false-RH reductio.

**Disposition:** RIGHTER DIRECTION / RH-CONDITIONAL / ONE ROW / NO ALIGNMENT.

## 3. Ki's microscopic logarithmic-derivative theorem remains RH-conditional

Haseo Ki proves, assuming RH, a microscopic law of the form

\[
\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
=
\frac1{s-\rho}
+
O(\log t)
\]

uniformly at \(1/\log T\)-scale under the corresponding derivative-zero
separation hypothesis.

This controls the full logarithmic derivative on the correct physical scale.

But:

- RH is explicit;
- the output is an unweighted nearest-pole approximation;
- it does not provide a derivative Cauchy frame;
- it does not control \(D_{\Tau,F}v\) for the KPH coefficient vector;
- it does not yield DD-BRIDGE.

**Disposition:** CORRECT SCALE / WRONG ASSUMPTION + WRONG WEIGHTED OBJECT.

Primary source:
H. Ki, *The zeros of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function near the
critical line*, arXiv:math/0701726.

## 4. Guo / Feng / Zhang / Soundararajan lineage does not cross the frame gate

The classical near-line literature studies:

- horizontal distribution of \(\zeta'\)-zeros;
- implications from close zeta gaps to nearby derivative zeros;
- restrictions on the number of derivative zeros in rectangles between
  consecutive zeta zeros;
- asymptotic distributions and positive proportions near the critical line.

No theorem located in this screen has the form

\[
\boxed{
\text{every prescribed dangerous }n=7\text{ zeta packet}
\Longrightarrow
n-1\text{ derivative zeros with projective
Cauchy--Vandermonde conditioning}.
}
\]

Nor was a theorem located guaranteeing one canonically chosen derivative zero
with a projective lower pairing

\[
|R_{v_F}(\tau(F))|\ge H_F^{-a}
\]

against the actual KPH soft ray.

**Disposition:** GEOMETRICALLY RELEVANT / FRAME-SOURCE GATE NOT CROSSED.

## 5. Farmer--Ki / derivative-zero correlation remains aggregate

Farmer--Ki type results relate a sufficiently large population of derivative
zeros near the line to a population of close zeta gaps.

Farmer--Gonek and related work study pair correlation/statistics of the
derivative divisor, typically under RH in the \(\Xi'\) setting.

These theorems can constrain global distributions of derivative zeros.

They do not assign a conditioned derivative frame to one prescribed dangerous
selected packet.

A density theorem also permits:

- local holes;
- severe clustering;
- repeated assignment of many derivative zeros to unrelated zeta packets;
- arbitrarily poor Cauchy-frame conditioning unless an additional local theorem
  is supplied.

**Disposition:** AGGREGATE / NO WORST-PACKET FRAME.

## 6. Critical-value and curvature literature still does not supply weighted alignment

Chorge studies values of

\[
|\zeta(\rho')|
\]

at critical points \(\zeta'(\rho')=0\).

Pearce-Crump and related work study moments of zeta/derivatives at critical-line
extrema or derivative-related sampling sets.

The outputs are value/moment statistics, not a pointwise relation

\[
R_v(\rho')
=
\sum_j\frac{v_j}{\rho'-\rho_j}
\]

for a prescribed KPH coefficient vector \(v\).

No unconditional every-critical-point theorem was located controlling

\[
\frac{\zeta''(\rho')}{\zeta(\rho')}
\]

at the strength needed to manufacture the missing KPH weights.

Thus the weighted-criticality mismatch from IRT-1B survives the source screen.

**Disposition:** ENRICHED VALUES EXIST / NO WEIGHTED KPH-CHANNEL THEOREM.

## 7. Recent 2025--2026 derivative moment results are averaged

The 2026 work of Durkan--Hughes--Pearce-Crump gives full asymptotics for
discrete second moments of mixed derivatives of zeta evaluated at zeta zeros,
including unconditional and conditional error terms.

This is significant information about derivative values on the original
divisor.

But its quantifier is a summed/discrete moment over many zeros. It does not
provide:

- a derivative-zero family \(\Tau(F)\) attached to every prescribed packet;
- a lower singular value of \(D_{\Tau,F}\);
- a weighted \(D_{\Tau,F}v_F\) lower bound;
- DD-BRIDGE.

The 2025 Hughes--Pearce-Crump work on complex moments is conjectural/random
matrix in nature rather than an unconditional packetwise frame theorem.

**Disposition:** RECENT / STRONG AVERAGE INFORMATION / WRONG QUANTIFIER.

## 8. Reciprocal derivative bounds do not supply the mixed frame

Chavez's current paper (arXiv:2409.02106, revised 2026 and accepted for
publication) relates logarithmic anticorrelation of multiplicative functions
to sums involving

\[
|\rho\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2}
\]

and possible effective upper bounds on \(1/|\zeta'(\rho)|\), under RH and
simplicity in the displayed identities.

Even a pointwise upper bound on \(1/|\zeta'(\rho)|\) would control residues or
simple-zero conditioning on the original divisor.

It would not by itself:

- produce \(\Xi'\)-zeros around each dangerous packet;
- control the derivative Cauchy--Vandermonde determinant;
- attach the derivative Cauchy frame to the KPH vector.

**Disposition:** ORIGINAL-DIVISOR CONDITIONING / NO MIXED-DIVISOR ALIGNMENT.

algebraically in IRT-1B.

Separate explicit formulas/statistics for \(Z(\Xi)\) and \(Z(\Xi')\) do not
supply the weighted transfer.

**Disposition:** NO HIT.

## 11. Why the frame and alignment clauses are logically distinct

Even if a future theorem supplies a derivative frame with

\[
\sigma_{\min}\left(
D_{\Tau,F}\big|_{{\bf1}^\perp}
\right)
\ge H_F^{-a},
\]

this does not imply the mixed alignment bridge.

The frame only says that the derivative sampling set can observe every
zero-moment selected coefficient vector.

It does not say KPH-soft vectors must produce *small* derivative observations.

Conversely, an upper factor-through bound without a frame is useless because
the derivative channel could itself be nearly blind.

Thus AZ-MIXED-CAUCHY-ALIGN genuinely needs two types of information:

\[
\boxed{
\text{derivative-frame observability}
+
\text{KPH-to-derivative alignment}.
}
\]

No located source supplies their combination.
