## 1. Nontriviality rule

A traversal is not complete merely because RH makes an off-axis negative morphology impossible.

The statement

[
RH \Longrightarrow \text{no off-axis defect packet}
]

is true at the coarse zero-location level but is not the object of this investigation.

For each branch we instead preserve the typed object at the stop line and ask:

1. what canonical object represents the same data under the RH-positive architecture;
2. what theorem constructs or constrains that object;
3. what theorem consumes it;
4. where RH first enters the dependency chain;
5. whether the project's missing interface lies before, at, or after that RH-sensitive edge.

This is the **positive-slot** question.

## 4. Three source-status rings

Every source hit must be classified before it is used.

### Ring E — RH-equivalent

The theorem is stated as equivalent to RH under its published hypotheses.

### Ring C — conditional consequence

The theorem is proved assuming RH, but is not itself asserted to be equivalent to RH.

### Ring A — compatible architecture

The construction is motivated by, compatible with, or useful for RH but is not a theorem consequence of RH merely from its existence.

No Ring-A construction may be silently promoted to Ring C or Ring E.
